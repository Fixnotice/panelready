Questions

Straight answers.

Where does the data come from?

The National Plan and Provider Enumeration System (NPPES), run by CMS. Every provider and organization that bills must have an NPI, and CMS publishes the full registry and weekly updates as public files. We read the weekly file; we don't change it or add to it.

How fresh is it?

CMS posts each week's file about a week after the week ends, and most registrations in it are one to six days old at that point. So a practice is on your list one to two weeks after it registered, which is typically weeks before it has finished credentialing with its first payer.

Are these real practices or just anyone with an NPI?

We keep organizations (practices, clinics, agencies, suppliers) and individuals who flagged themselves as sole proprietors. Employees registering for a new job, students and deactivated numbers are dropped. Roughly one row in three survives.

Do I get email addresses?

For organizations, where we can find one. The federal registry has phone numbers and addresses but no emails, so we look each new organization up on Google and on its own website and add the website, email and Google rating we find; we never guess an address. Sole proprietors rarely have a website yet, so their rows have the phone and address they gave CMS.

What's the "stuck in credentialing" list?

Once a month, subscribers also get the practices in their state that registered for an NPI 2 to 12 months ago, in specialties where a good share of new practices enroll with Medicare (nurse practitioners, physical therapists, physicians and the like), and still don't appear on Medicare's public enrollment list. Months in, they can't bill Medicare yet, which usually means credentialing hasn't started or has stalled. Some practices skip Medicare on purpose, so treat it as "likely", not certain.

What are the revalidation and practice-moved lists?

Every Medicare provider has to revalidate its enrollment every few years or Medicare can deactivate its billing, and CMS publishes the due dates. Once a month you get the practices in your state that are due in the next six months or already overdue, soonest first, with phone and owner. Every week you also get the practices whose registered location changed: each one has to update its address with Medicare and every insurance plan. Both are established practices that already bill, not start-ups.

How does the guarantee work?

If you don't sign at least one new client from your PanelReady lists during your first 90 days as a paying subscriber, reply to any of our emails and tell us. Your next 3 months are free: we pause your PayPal subscription for three months. One per company; no forms, no proof required.

Is my list exclusive to me?

No. PanelReady is a data subscription, not an exclusive-lead service: the Texas behavioral-health list is the same list for every company that subscribes to Texas behavioral health, and a new one arrives every week. In practice a state produces far more new practices each week than any one firm can call, and what you're paying for is being weeks ahead of everyone who isn't on the list. If you need a state and category reserved for your company alone, email us; that's a separate arrangement at a different price.

Yes: each state is its own plan, or email us for a bundle if you cover several.
