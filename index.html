How it works

The registry is public. The work is reading it every week and throwing away the 80% that isn't a practice.

1

We read the weekly federal file

Every NPI registered that week: 13,000-plus rows of providers, employees, students and practices, with specialty codes and addresses.

2

We keep the practices

Organizations and sole proprietors only, sorted into six categories by specialty code. Employees registering for a job, students and deactivated numbers are dropped.

3

You get a spreadsheet

Your state, your category: practice name, specialty, city, address, phone, NPI and registration date. Organizations first.

Questions

Where does the data come from?

The National Plan and Provider Enumeration System (NPPES), run by CMS. Every provider and organization that bills must have an NPI, and CMS publishes the full registry and weekly updates as public files. We read the weekly file; we don't change it.

How fresh is it?

CMS posts each week's file about a week after the week ends, and most registrations in it are one to six days old at that point. So a practice is on your list one to two weeks after it registered, which is typically weeks before it has finished credentialing.

Are these real practices or just anyone with an NPI?

We keep organizations (practices, clinics, agencies, suppliers) and individuals who flagged themselves as sole proprietors. Employees registering for a new job, students and deactivated numbers are dropped. Roughly one row in five survives.

Can I get more than one state?

Yes: each state is its own plan, or email us for a bundle if you cover several.
