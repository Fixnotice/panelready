Behavioral health led the week with 28, followed by home care & senior living (4) and medical practices & clinics (3). The busiest cities were Albuquerque (14), Rio Rancho (7), Las Cruces (3). That is 0.8% of the 4,935 new registrations nationwide, ranking New Mexico #32 of 52 states and territories this week.

At this week's pace New Mexico adds roughly 165 new practices a month. Every one of them has to be credentialed with payers and set up for billing in the months after it registers, which is why credentialing and medical-billing companies use this list as a call sheet: the practice is in the registry one to two weeks after it enrolled, usually before it has signed with anyone.

What stands out in New Mexico this week

New Mexico has about 2.1 million residents, so this week's 38 registrations work out to 1.8 new practices per 100,000 people, 24% above the national rate of 1.5. That is down 25% from the 51 registered the week before

The mix leans toward behavioral health: 74% of New Mexico's new practices this week against 39% nationally. 45% of the registrations are organizations rather than individual sole proprietors (nationally 55%); organizations are the ones most likely to hire out credentialing and billing.

Among neighboring states, Texas registered 439, Colorado registered 170, Arizona registered 151, Utah registered 78. New Mexico trails Texas and Colorado but is ahead of the rest.

The top three cities (Albuquerque, Rio Rancho, Las Cruces) account for 63% of the state's new practices; the rest are spread across smaller towns, which is where a weekly list earns its keep: nobody is driving past those offices to notice the new sign.

A practice that registers in New Mexico still has its paperwork ahead of it. Seeing Turquoise Care patients means enrolling with the state separately from the NPI, most commercial plans want a CAQH profile before they will credential anyone, and the whole cycle usually takes 60 to 120 days. That window, between the registry and the first claim, is when a New Mexico practice decides whether to do credentialing and billing in-house or hire it out, and it is why the practices on this page are worth a call this week rather than next quarter.

Questions about the New Mexico list

How many new healthcare practices open in New Mexico each month?

About 165 a month at the current pace (38 in the week ending October 25, 2026; 43 a week on average over the weeks tracked). The count includes new organizations and individuals who registered as sole proprietors; employees who registered for an NPI to take a job are excluded.

Where does this data come from?

The National Plan and Provider Enumeration System (NPPES), run by CMS. Every provider and organization that bills insurance needs an NPI, and CMS publishes the weekly registrations as a public file. PanelReady reads that file, keeps the practices and sole proprietors in New Mexico, sorts them into six categories and refreshes this page every week.

How fresh are the names on this page?

CMS posts each week's file about a week after the week ends, and most registrations in it are one to six days old at that point, so a practice shows up here one to two weeks after it registered. Subscribers get the full list the morning the file is posted.

What does a subscriber get that this page doesn't show?

Every New Mexico row, not a sample, with the complete phone number, street address, ZIP, NPI, taxonomy code, credential and authorized official, as a spreadsheet every Monday. PanelReady does not sell email addresses; the registry doesn't contain them.
