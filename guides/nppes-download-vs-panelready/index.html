NPPES download vs. PanelReady: build it or get it

By Matt, PanelReady's founder · Numbers from the week ending November 8, 2026

Short answer: Both start from the same free CMS file. If you're comfortable with a 300-column CSV (or a script) and want every field, download the NPPES weekly file and filter it yourself: it costs nothing. If you want the finished list every week, filtered to your territory, sorted best-first and with websites and emails for organizations, PanelReady costs $49 to $499 a month, and two lists are free to try.

When doing it yourself makes sense

  • You need fields PanelReady doesn't include, or every state at once for research.
  • Someone on your team writes scripts and can spend a few hours building the filter once (the steps are here).
  • You only need a list now and then.

When PanelReady makes sense

  • You want to call the right practices every week without building or maintaining anything.
  • You want contact details beyond the registry's phone number.
  • You sell credentialing and want the Medicare lists (stuck in credentialing, revalidation due) that take several more CMS files to build. See the four signals.

Other options

The NPI Registry search and API are free for looking up providers you already know, but they can't filter by registration date. Commercial healthcare provider databases are built for breadth across established providers rather than for the week a practice registers.

Questions

Is PanelReady's data different from NPPES?

No. PanelReady uses CMS's NPPES weekly file as published and doesn't alter records. It filters and sorts them and adds lookups (websites, emails, Google ratings, an OIG exclusion check) on top.

How much does PanelReady cost?

$49 a month for a local area, $89 for one state and one category, $149 for one state with every category, $199 for three states and $499 for every state. Yearly billing is the price of ten months, and two lists are free with no card.

Can I open the NPPES weekly file in Excel?

Yes, but it's a ZIP of large CSV files with more than 300 columns, so filtering it by hand takes a while. The monthly full file is too big for Excel.
