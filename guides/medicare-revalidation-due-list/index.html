The Medicare revalidation due date list, and how to use it

By Matt, PanelReady's founder · Numbers from the week ending November 8, 2026

Short answer: CMS publishes the Revalidation Due Date List on data.cms.gov, with each Medicare-enrolled provider's next revalidation due date. Due dates appear about six to seven months ahead (providers further out show TBD). Most providers revalidate every five years and DMEPOS suppliers every three, and missing the date can get Medicare billing deactivated. Filter the list by state and a due date in the next six months to get practices with a deadline. PanelReady's November 2026 list has 53,257 of them nationwide.

What's on the list

One row per Medicare enrollment: the provider's name and NPI, the enrollment state, the provider type and specialty, the revalidation due date (and an adjusted due date when CMS moved it), and reassignment counts: how many groups an individual bills through, or how many providers bill through a group. It has no phone number or address; those come from the provider's NPPES record. CMS updates it monthly.

Who has to revalidate, and when

Every provider and supplier enrolled in Medicare: every five years for most, every three years for DMEPOS suppliers, and off-cycle whenever CMS asks. CMS sets each due date at the end of a month and lists it about six to seven months ahead. A provider that doesn't revalidate on time can have its payments held and its enrollment deactivated, and services during a deactivation may not be payable.

How to turn it into a call list

  1. Download the newest Revalidation Due Date List from data.cms.gov (or query it through CMS's data API).
  2. Keep enrollments in your state with a due date between today and six months out (the adjusted date when there is one), plus the recently overdue.
  3. Keep one row per NPI. Individuals often have several enrollments; keep the soonest date.
  4. Leave out hospitals and large systems with their own credentialing staff. A high reassignment count is a good tell.
  5. Match each NPI to its NPPES record for the phone, the address and, for organizations, the authorized official.
  6. Call soonest-due first and mention the date: it's public, it's specific, and it's the reason you're calling.

Questions

How often do Medicare providers need to revalidate?

Every five years for most providers and suppliers, and every three years for DMEPOS suppliers. CMS can also ask for an off-cycle revalidation.

Where can I find a provider's Medicare revalidation due date?

In CMS's Revalidation Due Date List on data.cms.gov, searchable by NPI or name. A due date of TBD means CMS hasn't set one yet, usually because it's more than about seven months away.

What happens if a provider misses its revalidation date?

Medicare can hold its payments and deactivate its enrollment. It then has to submit a complete revalidation to be reactivated, and services during the deactivation may not be payable.

Is the Medicare revalidation list public?

Yes. CMS publishes it as open data on data.cms.gov, updated monthly and free to download.
