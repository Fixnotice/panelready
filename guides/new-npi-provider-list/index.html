How to get a list of newly registered NPI providers

By Matt, PanelReady's founder · Numbers from the week ending November 8, 2026

Short answer: CMS publishes every new NPI registration in a free weekly file, the NPPES Weekly Update on download.cms.gov. Download the ZIP, keep the records whose Provider Enumeration Date falls in that week, keep organizations and sole proprietors, then filter by practice-location state and taxonomy code. That's the list. PanelReady does it every week and emails it as a spreadsheet: the week ending November 8, 2026 had 4,952 new practices and sole proprietors nationwide, 2,599 of them organizations.

Where new NPI registrations are published

The National Plan and Provider Enumeration System (NPPES), run by CMS, issues every National Provider Identifier and publishes the registry as public data in three downloads on its NPI files page:

  • Weekly Update (the weekly incremental file): every NPI record created or changed that week, a few megabytes zipped. This is the one you want for new registrations.
  • Monthly file: the entire registry, every NPI ever issued. Over 1 GB zipped and many times that unzipped, far too big for Excel.
  • Monthly deactivation file: the NPIs deactivated that month.

The NPI Registry search and API are built for looking up a provider you already know. They search by name, number, location and specialty but not by registration date, so they can't tell you who registered last week, and a single search returns at most 1,200 results.

How to turn the weekly file into a list of new practices

  1. Download the newest Weekly Update ZIP (Version 2) and open the main npidata_pfile CSV: one row per NPI, more than 300 columns.
  2. Keep rows whose Provider Enumeration Date falls inside the week. The others are existing NPIs that changed something (an address, a specialty). Those are useful in their own right, but they aren't new.
  3. Keep Entity Type Code 2 (organizations: practices, clinics, agencies, suppliers) and individuals whose Is Sole Proprietor is Y. Other individuals are mostly employees registering for a new job; they don't buy credentialing or billing themselves. Roughly one new NPI in three survives this step.
  4. Filter on Provider Business Practice Location Address State Name for your territory. The mailing address is often a home or a billing company.
  5. Take the specialty from the Healthcare Provider Taxonomy Code whose Primary Taxonomy Switch is Y and translate it with the NUCC taxonomy list (101YM0800X, for example, is a mental health counselor).
  6. Drop any row with an NPI Deactivation Date.

What a new-registration record contains, and what it doesn't

Each record has the legal name, the practice-location and mailing addresses, the practice phone (often a fax too), the taxonomy codes and license numbers, the enumeration date and, for organizations, the authorized official's name, title and phone: usually the owner or administrator. It has no email address and no website; those you have to find yourself.

A practice usually shows up one to two weeks after it registered: CMS posts each week's file a few days after the week ends. That is typically before it has finished enrolling with Medicare, Medicaid or commercial plans, which is why credentialing and billing companies watch this file.

Questions

Is NPPES data free to use?

Yes. CMS publishes the NPPES registry as public data that anyone can download free from download.cms.gov/nppes. It includes names, addresses, phone numbers and specialties, but no email addresses.

How often is the NPPES file updated?

CMS posts a Weekly Update file every week, the full registry once a month, and a monthly file of deactivated NPIs.

How soon after registering does a new practice appear in the file?

Usually one to two weeks. CMS posts each week's file a few days after the week ends, and most registrations in it are less than a week old.

Can I search the NPI Registry by registration date?

No. The NPI Registry website and API search by name, NPI, location and specialty, not by enumeration date. To find new registrations you need the weekly download file.

Does the NPI registry include email addresses?

No. Records have addresses and phone numbers but no email or website. PanelReady looks up websites, emails and Google ratings for new organizations from public sources.
